Human evolution & prehistory

Out of Africa migration

A small population of Homo sapiens left Africa, probably across the Bab-el-Mandeb strait or through Sinai, and spread along the southern coasts of Arabia and India to Southeast Asia and Australia within about 15,000 years. They met and interbred with Neanderthals and Denisovans, which is why most people outside Africa carry about 2% Neanderthal DNA. Ancient DNA and stone tools from sites such as Jwalapuram in India help map the route.

Human evolution & prehistory

Peopling of the Americas

When sea levels were low in the Ice Age, Beringia linked Siberia and Alaska. People crossed and moved south along the Pacific coast or through an ice-free corridor; footprints at White Sands, New Mexico, suggest presence by about 21,000 years ago, and Monte Verde in Chile was occupied by 14,500 years ago. Within a few thousand years humans reached Tierra del Fuego.

Ancient world

Bantu expansion

Farmers speaking Bantu languages spread from the Cameroon–Nigeria borderlands across central, eastern and southern Africa over 2,000 years, carrying yams, sorghum, cattle and, later, ironworking. Today about 350 million people speak some 500 Bantu languages, including Swahili, Zulu and Kikongo, and genetic studies trace the movement.

Ancient world

Austronesian expansion

Austronesian-speaking farmers sailed out of Taiwan to the Philippines and Indonesia, then east into the Pacific as the Lapita culture and west to Madagascar, using outrigger canoes. They carried rice, pigs, chickens and taro. Their languages, from Malagasy to Hawaiian, now span half the globe's longitudes.

Ancient world

Indo-European migrations

Ancient DNA shows that pastoralists of the Yamnaya culture, using wagons and horses, moved into Europe around 3000 BCE, and related steppe groups reached Central Asia and later South Asia around 2000–1500 BCE, mixing with local populations. The spread explains why Sanskrit, Greek, Latin, Persian and English share many words.

Ancient world

Sea Peoples' raids

Coalitions of seafaring raiders attacked Anatolia, Cyprus, the Levant and Egypt; Ramesses III recorded his victory over them at Medinet Habu around 1175 BCE. Their identity is still debated. Along with drought, earthquakes and rebellions, they helped bring down the Hittite empire, Ugarit and Mycenaean palaces.

Ancient & classical turning points

Kurukshetra and the Mahajanapadas

Clans became 16 mahajanapadas, kingdoms and republics such as Magadha, Kosala, Vajji and Gandhara, with cities, coins and armies. Republics (ganas) like the Vajji were governed by assemblies. This was the world the Buddha and Mahavira lived in.

Ancient world

Third Buddhist Council and missions

Ashoka sponsored a council and sent missionaries, including his son Mahinda to Sri Lanka, where the Pali Canon was later written down. Buddhism then spread along trade routes to Central Asia, China and Southeast Asia.

Ancient world

Council of Nicaea

Emperor Constantine called about 300 bishops to settle the Arian controversy over the nature of Christ; they issued the Nicene Creed, still recited by most Christians, and set the method of dating Easter.

Ancient world

Nalanda founded

Founded under the Gupta king Kumaragupta I, Nalanda grew to host thousands of students and teachers in Buddhist philosophy, logic, grammar, medicine and astronomy; Xuanzang and Yijing studied there. It flourished for 700 years until destroyed around 1193.

Medieval world

Spread of Islam to India via Kerala

Arab traders had long visited the Malabar coast for pepper; tradition dates the Cheraman Juma mosque at Kodungallur to 629 CE. Islam spread peacefully among coastal communities, forming the Mappila Muslims, long before conquests in the north.

Medieval world

Tang–Abbasid paper transfer

After the Battle of Talas, papermaking spread to Samarkand and then Baghdad (794), replacing papyrus and parchment, enabling the Islamic Golden Age's books and later Europe's printing.

Medieval world

Founding of Baghdad and the House of Wisdom

Caliph al-Mansur founded the Round City of Baghdad; under al-Mamun the House of Wisdom translated Greek, Persian and Indian works, and scholars such as al-Khwarizmi developed algebra and adopted Indian numerals.

Medieval world

Norse reach North America

Leif Erikson's Norse settlers built a camp at L'Anse aux Meadows, confirmed by excavation in the 1960s; tree rings date woodcutting there to 1021.

Medieval world

Rajendra Chola's Ganga expedition

Rajendra I marched to the Ganga and built Gangaikonda Cholapuram, then sent a fleet to raid Srivijaya's ports (1025), the most ambitious overseas campaign by an Indian state.

Medieval world

Great Zimbabwe at its height

Great Zimbabwe's stone enclosures housed perhaps 18,000 people, trading gold to the coast for Chinese porcelain and Persian glass.

Medieval world

Mansa Musa's hajj

Mansa Musa travelled with thousands of attendants and camels laden with gold; his gifts in Cairo reportedly depressed gold's value for years. The 1375 Catalan Atlas shows him holding a gold nugget.

Medieval world

Ottoman siege of Vienna

Suleiman the Magnificent besieged Vienna in 1529 but withdrew; a second siege in 1683 was broken by Jan Sobieski's Polish cavalry, beginning Ottoman retreat.

Early modern world

Columbian Exchange

Potatoes, maize, tomatoes, chillies and cacao went to the Old World; wheat, horses, cattle and diseases went to the Americas, where epidemics killed most of the indigenous population.

Early modern world

Ended the Thirty Years' War and the Eighty Years' War, recognising state sovereignty and religious coexistence.

Early modern world

Modern world
