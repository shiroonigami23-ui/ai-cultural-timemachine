East Asia Through Time

China, Korea, Japan and Mongolia from Peking Man and the first rice and millet farmers to the dynasties, the Mongol empire, the Opium Wars and the modern era.

📅 Reviewed 2026-11-15⏱ 1 min read🔗 2 sources🎓 Introductory
~780,000 yr ago: 'Peking Man' (Homo erectus) at Zhoukoudian.
~18,000 BCE: Oldest pottery (Xianrendong).
~7000 BCE: Rice (Yangtze) and millet (Yellow River) farming.
~14,000–300 BCE: Jōmon culture in Japan.
~1600–1046 BCE: Shang dynasty; oracle bones.
~551 BCE: Confucius born.
221 BCE: Qin unifies China.
206 BCE – 220 CE: Han dynasty; paper; Silk Road.
~300 BCE onward: Yayoi rice farmers in Japan.
618–907: Tang golden age.
960–1279: Song: gunpowder, compass, printing.
1206: Genghis Khan.
1368–1644: Ming; Zheng He's voyages.
1443: Hangul created in Korea.
1603–1868: Tokugawa Japan.
1839–1860: Opium Wars.
1868: Meiji Restoration.
1911: End of imperial China.
1949: People's Republic of China.
1950–53: Korean War.

Technology and knowledge#

China gave the world silk, paper, porcelain, the compass, gunpowder, printing, cast iron, the crossbow and the seismoscope; Korea metal movable type (Jikji, 1377) and the scientific Hangul alphabet; Japan refined woodblock printing, swordsmithing and later rapid industrialisation.

How people lived#

Most East Asians were farmers growing rice in the south and millet and wheat in the north, organised around families, ancestors and Confucian duties. Scholar-officials chosen by exams ran the Chinese state from the Sui–Tang era to 1905.

Sources: Wikipedia: History of China · Wikipedia: History of Japan
