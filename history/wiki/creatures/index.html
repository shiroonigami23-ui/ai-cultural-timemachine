🐉 Legendary & Mythical Creatures

Dragons, griffins, centaurs, Medusa, the Hydra, cyclopes, the Chimera and Minotaur; Leviathan, djinn and the golem; trolls, goblins, vampires, werewolves, the kraken and unicorn; rakshasas, the brahmarakshasa, vetala, nagas and Garuda; Chinese dragons, oni and kitsune; the yeti, Bigfoot and the Loch Ness Monster. For each: when and where it is first recorded, what it looks like, where it lives and what it eats, its powers, the stories it appears in and the heroes who fought it — and a straight answer on whether anything like it could exist, from fossils and real animals to hoaxes and DNA tests.

43 entries in 6 groups.

📊 Charts and statistics for this catalog
Greek & Roman

Centaur

Upper body of a human joined at the waist to the body and four legs of a horse. First attested: Clay figurine from Lefkandi, Euboea (c. 900 BCE); named in Homer's Iliad (8th century BCE).

Greek & Roman

Griffin

Body, tail and hind legs of a lion; head, wings and talons of an eagle. First attested: Seals and art from Susa and Mesopotamia (c. 3000 BCE); Greek accounts from Aristeas of Proconnesus (7th century BCE), retold by Herodotus.

Greek & Roman

Hydra

A giant water serpent with many heads (often nine); one head was immortal. First attested: Hesiod's Theogony (c. 700 BCE).

Greek & Roman

Medusa and the Gorgons

Women with living snakes for hair, sometimes with tusks, wings and bronze hands; Medusa was the only mortal of the three Gorgon sisters. First attested: Hesiod's Theogony (c. 700 BCE); Gorgon faces (gorgoneia) on temples and shields from the 7th century BCE.

Greek & Roman

Cyclops

Giants with a single eye in the middle of the forehead. First attested: Hesiod's Theogony and Homer's Odyssey (c. 8th–7th century BCE).

Greek & Roman

Chimera

A fire-breathing lion with a goat's head rising from its back and a serpent for a tail. First attested: Homer's Iliad, Book 6 (8th century BCE).

Greek & Roman

Minotaur

A man with the head and tail of a bull. First attested: Greek vase paintings from the 7th century BCE; myth set in Bronze Age Crete.

Greek & Roman

Pegasus

A white winged horse. First attested: Hesiod's Theogony (c. 700 BCE).

Greek & Roman

Cerberus

A giant dog with three heads (Hesiod says fifty), a serpent tail and snakes growing from its back. First attested: Hesiod's Theogony (c. 700 BCE).

Greek & Roman

Sphinx

Egyptian: a lion with a human (pharaoh's) head. Greek: a lion's body, eagle's wings and a woman's head. First attested: Great Sphinx of Giza (c. 2500 BCE); Greek sphinx in Hesiod (c. 700 BCE).

Greek & Roman

Basilisk

A small serpent with a crown-like mark; medieval versions became a rooster-serpent (cockatrice). First attested: Pliny the Elder's Natural History (77 CE).

Norse, Celtic & European folklore

Dragon

A giant winged reptile with four legs, scales, claws and fiery breath (in medieval Europe). First attested: Mesopotamian serpent-dragons such as the mušḫuššu (from c. 2100 BCE) and Tiamat in the Enuma Elish; Greek drakon from Homer; medieval fire-breathing dragon in Beowulf (c. 700–1000 CE).

Near East & Biblical

Leviathan

A colossal sea serpent or dragon with impenetrable scales and fiery breath (Job 41). First attested: Ugaritic Baal Cycle (c. 1400 BCE) as Lotan; Hebrew Bible (Job 41, Psalm 74, Isaiah 27:1).

Near East & Biblical

Djinn (Jinn)

Invisible beings who can take human or animal form; in folklore, ghostly shapes of smoke or fire. First attested: Pre-Islamic Arabian poetry and belief; the Quran (7th century CE), especially Surah al-Jinn (72).

Near East & Biblical

Golem

A human figure made of clay or mud. First attested: Babylonian Talmud, Sanhedrin 65b (c. 5th century CE): the sage Rava creates a man; Sefer Yetzirah; Prague legend from the 19th century.

Near East & Biblical

Giants

Humans of enormous size; Norse giants could be monstrous or beautiful. First attested: Mesopotamian Humbaba in Gilgamesh (c. 2100 BCE); Nephilim in Genesis 6 and Goliath in 1 Samuel; Greek Gigantes and Titans in Hesiod (c. 700 BCE); Norse jötnar in the Eddas.

Norse, Celtic & European folklore

Goblin

Small, ugly, mischievous humanoids, often green or grey. First attested: 'Gobelinus', a spirit in Orderic Vitalis's chronicle (12th century); later European folklore.

Norse, Celtic & European folklore

Troll

In Norse sources, giant-like beings; in later folklore, huge, ugly creatures with big noses, or small and hairy. First attested: Old Norse sagas and the Prose Edda (13th century).

Norse, Celtic & European folklore

Wyvern

A dragon with two legs and two wings, and a barbed tail. First attested: Medieval European heraldry (the word dates from the 17th century, from Old French 'wivre', a viper).

Norse, Celtic & European folklore

Vampire

In folklore, a bloated, ruddy corpse; after Bram Stoker, a pale, elegant aristocrat. First attested: Slavic and Balkan folklore; the word appears in Habsburg reports from Serbia in the 1720s (Petar Blagojević 1725, Arnold Paole 1726–1732).

Norse, Celtic & European folklore

Werewolf

A human who transforms into a wolf or wolf-human hybrid. First attested: Ovid's Metamorphoses (8 CE) tells of King Lycaon turned into a wolf; Petronius's Satyricon (1st century CE).

Norse, Celtic & European folklore

Unicorn

A white horse or goat-like animal with a single spiralled horn. First attested: Greek physician Ctesias's account of India (c. 400 BCE); a one-horned animal appears on Indus Valley seals (c. 2600–1900 BCE).

Norse, Celtic & European folklore

Kraken

A colossal octopus or squid large enough to be mistaken for an island. First attested: Erik Pontoppidan's Natural History of Norway (1752); Norse sagas mention sea monsters.

Norse, Celtic & European folklore

Gargoyle

Grotesque stone creatures on cathedral roofs. First attested: Gothic church water spouts from the 13th century; legend of La Gargouille of Rouen.

Norse, Celtic & European folklore

Banshee

A woman with long hair, washing bloodstained clothes or wailing. First attested: Irish folklore (bean sídhe, 'woman of the fairy mound'); 14th-century texts.

South Asian

Rakshasa

Fierce humanoids with fangs, claws and sometimes many heads; masters of illusion who can take beautiful forms. First attested: Rigveda (c. 1500–1200 BCE), where Agni is invoked to burn the rakshas; central in the Ramayana and Mahabharata.

South Asian

Brahmarakshasa

A terrifying spirit with a Brahmin's sacred thread and a demon's form. First attested: Puranic and folk traditions (first millennium CE); Kerala temple traditions.

South Asian

Vetala (Betaal)

A spirit that possesses corpses, hanging upside down from trees in cremation grounds. First attested: Vetala Panchavimshati ('25 tales of the Vetala'), included in Somadeva's Kathasaritsagara (11th century).

South Asian

Naga

Serpents, often cobras, sometimes many-headed or half-human. First attested: Vedic and early Buddhist texts (first millennium BCE); serpent deities in Indus seals are possible precursors.

South Asian

Garuda

A giant eagle-like bird, sometimes with a human body. First attested: Mahabharata (Adi Parva, compiled c. 400 BCE–400 CE).

South Asian

Makara

A sea creature combining a crocodile, fish and elephant trunk. First attested: Sculptures from the 2nd century BCE (Bharhut, Sanchi); Vedic texts.

East Asian

Chinese dragon (Long)

A long scaly serpent with four legs, deer antlers, whiskers and no wings. First attested: Dragon figure of clam shells in a tomb at Xishuipo, Henan (c. 4500 BCE); Hongshan jade 'pig-dragons'.

East Asian

Phoenix

A magnificent red and gold bird like an eagle. First attested: Herodotus's Histories, Book 2 (c. 430 BCE), drawing on the Egyptian bennu bird of Heliopolis.

East Asian

Oni

Huge ogres with red or blue skin, horns, tusks, tiger-skin loincloths and iron clubs. First attested: Heian-period Japanese literature (from the 9th–10th centuries).

East Asian

Kitsune

Foxes with up to nine tails, who can take human form. First attested: Chinese nine-tailed fox in the Shan Hai Jing (c. 4th century BCE); Japanese kitsune in the Nihon Ryōiki (c. 822).

East Asian

Tengu

Bird-like or long-nosed red-faced mountain beings. First attested: Nihon Shoki (720 CE).

Americas, Africa & modern cryptids

Yeti

A large ape-like creature covered in hair. First attested: Sherpa folklore; 'metoh-kangmi' mistranslated as 'Abominable Snowman' after the 1921 Everest expedition.

Americas, Africa & modern cryptids

Bigfoot (Sasquatch)

A 2–3 m tall hairy ape-like creature. First attested: Coast Salish 'Sasq'ets' traditions; the name 'Bigfoot' from 1958 footprints in California.

Americas, Africa & modern cryptids

Loch Ness Monster

A long-necked creature like a plesiosaur. First attested: Adomnán's Life of St Columba (c. 700 CE) describes a 'water beast' in the River Ness in 565.

Americas, Africa & modern cryptids

Wendigo

A gaunt, ice-hearted giant, always hungry. First attested: Algonquian peoples' oral traditions (Ojibwe, Cree, Innu); recorded by Jesuit missionaries in the 17th century.

Americas, Africa & modern cryptids

Thunderbird

A gigantic bird. First attested: Indigenous oral traditions of the Pacific Northwest and Great Plains.

Americas, Africa & modern cryptids

Mermaid

Upper body of a woman, tail of a fish. First attested: Syrian goddess Atargatis, depicted half-fish (c. 1st millennium BCE); Greek sirens were originally bird-women.

Americas, Africa & modern cryptids

Chupacabra

Spiky-backed creature; later, hairless dog-like animals. First attested: Puerto Rico, 1995 (Madelyne Tolentino's sighting).
