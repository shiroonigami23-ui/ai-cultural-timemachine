🪷 East India Company rule

1757–1858

A private company ruled India with its own army of ~260,000.

When1757–1858
RegionSouth Asia
AreaIndia
Capital(s)Calcutta
Known forPlassey, revenue extraction, Permanent Settlement

Overview#

A private company ruled India with its own army of ~260,000.

Government and society#

A joint-stock company governed by directors in London, supervised from 1773 by Parliament, ruled through a Governor-General in Calcutta and presidencies in Madras and Bombay, with a private army of Indian sepoys.

How people lived#

Under Company rule, peasants faced heavy revenue demands through the Permanent Settlement, Ryotwari and Mahalwari systems, and many lost land to moneylenders. Bengal’s famous weavers were forced to sell cloth cheaply to Company agents, and later British machine-made textiles ruined them. Famines such as Bengal’s in 1770 killed millions.

Culture, beliefs and heritage#

British orientalists founded the Asiatic Society (1784), translated Sanskrit texts and promoted study of India’s past, while Christian missionaries and reformers like Ram Mohan Roy debated sati, caste and education. Macaulay’s 1835 Minute pushed English education, creating a new educated class.

Food#

Indians ate traditional grains and pulses, but Company revenue demands contributed to famines such as the Bengal famine of 1770, which killed about a third of the province’s people.

Construction and architecture#

Fort William in Calcutta, Fort St George in Madras and neo-classical government buildings.

Technology and knowledge#

Surveys such as the Great Trigonometrical Survey (from 1802), which later measured Everest; steamships and the first telegraph lines.

How it rose#

Victory at Plassey (1757) and the diwani of Bengal (1765) turned the Company into a territorial power; wars against Mysore, the Marathas and Sikhs extended its rule.

How it declined or fell#

The 1857 uprising led Parliament to abolish Company rule in 1858.

Legacy#

Colonial administration, the drain of wealth, and the deindustrialisation of Indian textiles.
