👤 People of History

Rulers and conquerors, freedom fighters, philosophers, scientists, poets and reformers from every age, with India's in depth.

119 entries in 3 groups.

📊 Charts and statistics for this catalog
Rulers & conquerors

Hammurabi

Sixth king of the First Dynasty of Babylon (r. c. 1792–1750 BCE), he turned a small city-state into an empire covering most of Mesopotamia through diplomacy and war against Larsa, Eshnunna and Mari. Late in his reign he set up stone stelae carrying 282 laws, framed as given by the sun god Shamash, so that 'the strong may not oppress the weak'. His letters show a king closely involved in canals, taxes and court cases.

Rulers & conquerors

Ramesses II

The 'Great Ancestor' of later pharaohs, Ramesses II reigned for 66 years (c. 1279–1213 BCE). He fought the Hittites at Kadesh (c. 1274 BCE), proclaimed it a victory, and later signed the world's oldest surviving peace treaty with them. He built Abu Simbel, the Ramesseum and a new capital, Pi-Ramesses, and fathered over a hundred children.

Rulers & conquerors

Hatshepsut

Daughter of Thutmose I, Hatshepsut ruled first as regent for her stepson Thutmose III and then as pharaoh (c. 1479–1458 BCE), depicted with the royal false beard. Her reign was peaceful and prosperous: she sent an expedition to the land of Punt for incense and built the terraced temple at Deir el-Bahari. After her death her images were chiselled away, perhaps to secure Thutmose III's line.

Rulers & conquerors

Cyrus the Great

Founder of the Achaemenid Persian Empire (r. c. 559–530 BCE), Cyrus overthrew the Medes, conquered Lydia and took Babylon in 539 BCE almost without a fight. The Cyrus Cylinder records that he returned deported peoples and restored their temples; the Bible praises him for letting the Jews return to Jerusalem. He died campaigning in Central Asia and was buried in a simple tomb at Pasargadae.

Rulers & conquerors

Alexander the Great

King of Macedon at 20, Alexander (356–323 BCE) was taught by Aristotle and in 11 years conquered the Persian Empire, Egypt and lands to the Indus, never losing a major battle. At the Hydaspes (326 BCE) he defeated Porus, but his exhausted army refused to go beyond the Beas. He founded cities, mixed Greek and Persian elites and spread Greek culture, but died in Babylon at 32, and his empire split among his generals.

Rulers & conquerors

Chandragupta Maurya

With the guidance of his mentor Chanakya, Chandragupta (r. c. 322–297 BCE) overthrew the Nanda dynasty and founded the Maurya Empire, the first to unite most of the Indian subcontinent. He defeated Seleucus I, who ceded the northwest in exchange for 500 elephants. Jain tradition says he abdicated, became a monk under Bhadrabahu and fasted to death at Shravanabelagola.

Rulers & conquerors

Ashoka

Grandson of Chandragupta, Ashoka (r. c. 268–232 BCE) conquered Kalinga in a war that his own edict says killed 100,000 and deported 150,000. Filled with remorse, he embraced Buddhism and dhamma — non-violence, tolerance and welfare — building hospitals, wells and rest houses and sending missions abroad. His lion capital at Sarnath is India's national emblem and his wheel is on its flag.

Rulers & conquerors

Qin Shi Huang

King Ying Zheng of Qin conquered the six rival Warring States and in 221 BCE proclaimed himself First Emperor. He standardised script, coins, weights and roads, joined border walls into an early Great Wall, and ordered the burning of books and the persecution of scholars. Obsessed with immortality, he died in 210 BCE and was buried with the Terracotta Army.

Rulers & conquerors

Julius Caesar

A Roman general and politician (100–44 BCE), Caesar conquered Gaul, crossed the Rubicon in 49 BCE to start a civil war, and became dictator. He reformed the calendar (the Julian calendar) and expanded the Senate. Senators who feared he would become king stabbed him on the Ides of March, 44 BCE; his heir Octavian became Rome's first emperor.

Rulers & conquerors

Augustus

Born Octavian, Caesar's adopted heir defeated Antony and Cleopatra at Actium (31 BCE) and in 27 BCE became Augustus, the first Roman emperor. He kept republican forms while holding real power, began 200 years of the Pax Romana, built roads and temples, and said he found Rome brick and left it marble. The month of August is named after him.

Rulers & conquerors

Cleopatra

Cleopatra VII (69–30 BCE), last of the Ptolemies and the only one to learn Egyptian, allied with Julius Caesar and then Mark Antony to preserve Egypt's independence. After their defeat at Actium, she took her own life rather than be paraded in Rome. Egypt became a Roman province.

Rulers & conquerors

Kanishka

The greatest Kushan emperor (r. c. 127–150 CE), Kanishka ruled from Bactria to the Ganga with capitals at Purushapura (Peshawar) and Mathura. A patron of Buddhism, he convened the Fourth Buddhist Council in Kashmir and supported Gandhara art. His coins show Greek, Persian, Indian and Buddhist gods.

Rulers & conquerors

Samudragupta

The Gupta emperor (r. c. 335–375 CE) described in the Allahabad pillar inscription as having defeated kings across north India and made southern rulers tributaries. He also performed the horse sacrifice and was depicted on coins playing the veena, showing his love of music and poetry.

Rulers & conquerors

Justinian I

Byzantine emperor (r. 527–565) who tried to restore the Roman Empire, reconquering North Africa and Italy. He codified Roman law in the Corpus Juris Civilis, rebuilt Hagia Sophia, and survived the Nika riots thanks to his wife Theodora. His reign was also struck by the Plague of Justinian.

Rulers & conquerors

Harsha

Harshavardhana (r. 606–647) unified north India from Kannauj, wrote Sanskrit plays (Ratnavali, Nagananda), and hosted the Chinese pilgrim Xuanzang. Pulakeshin II stopped his southern expansion at the Narmada.

Rulers & conquerors

Charlemagne

King of the Franks (r. 768–814), crowned emperor by Pope Leo III in 800, he united much of western Europe, conquered the Saxons and Lombards, and sponsored the Carolingian Renaissance of learning and script.

Rulers & conquerors

Rajaraja Chola I

Chola emperor (r. 985–1014) who conquered the Cheras, Pandyas and northern Sri Lanka, built a navy, carried out a land survey, and built the Brihadeeswarar temple at Thanjavur (1010).

Rulers & conquerors

Genghis Khan

Born Temüjin (c. 1162–1227), he united the Mongol tribes in 1206 and conquered from China to Persia, creating the largest land empire. He used merit-based promotion, a postal system and religious tolerance, but his conquests killed millions.

Rulers & conquerors

Razia Sultan

The only woman to rule the Delhi Sultanate (1236–1240), she wore a man's clothes, led armies and appointed non-Turkic officials, angering nobles who overthrew and killed her.

Rulers & conquerors

Mansa Musa

Emperor of Mali (r. c. 1312–1337), perhaps the richest person in history. His hajj in 1324 made Mali famous, and he built mosques and libraries in Timbuktu.

Rulers & conquerors

Krishnadevaraya

Vijayanagara emperor (r. 1509–1529) who defeated the Deccan sultans and Gajapatis, patronised Telugu and Kannada poets (the Ashtadiggajas) and wrote Amuktamalyada.

Rulers & conquerors

Babur

A descendant of Timur and Genghis Khan, Babur lost his homeland Fergana, took Kabul, and defeated Ibrahim Lodi at Panipat (1526) with field guns, founding the Mughal Empire. His memoir, the Baburnama, is frank and vivid.

Rulers & conquerors

Akbar

The greatest Mughal emperor (r. 1556–1605), Akbar expanded the empire across north India, abolished the jizya tax on non-Muslims, married Rajput princesses, held interfaith debates in the Ibadat Khana and created a revenue system under Todar Mal.

Rulers & conquerors

Shah Jahan

Mughal emperor (r. 1628–1658) who built the Taj Mahal for his wife Mumtaz Mahal, the Red Fort and Jama Masjid in Delhi. His son Aurangzeb imprisoned him in Agra Fort.

Rulers & conquerors

Elizabeth I

Queen of England (r. 1558–1603), she restored Protestantism, defeated the Spanish Armada and presided over a golden age of literature, including Shakespeare. The East India Company was chartered under her in 1600.

Rulers & conquerors

Chhatrapati Shivaji

Founder of the Maratha state (1630–1680), Shivaji built hill forts, a navy and an efficient administration, fought Bijapur and the Mughals and was crowned Chhatrapati in 1674.

Rulers & conquerors

Peter the Great

Tsar of Russia (r. 1682–1725) who travelled incognito to western Europe, modernised the army, built a navy and founded St Petersburg.

Rulers & conquerors

Maharaja Ranjit Singh

The 'Lion of Punjab' (1780–1839) united Sikh misls and built a strong, secular empire with a modern army.

Rulers & conquerors

Tipu Sultan

The 'Tiger of Mysore' (1750–1799) fought the British in four wars, used iron rockets and died defending Srirangapatna.

Rulers & conquerors

Napoleon Bonaparte

French general who became emperor (1804), conquered much of Europe and created the Napoleonic Code, before defeat at Waterloo (1815).

Rulers & conquerors

Queen Victoria

Queen of the UK (r. 1837–1901) and Empress of India from 1877, whose reign saw industrial and imperial expansion.

Freedom fighters & leaders

Rani Lakshmibai

Queen of Jhansi (c. 1828–1858) who fought the British in the 1857 rebellion after they annexed her kingdom under the Doctrine of Lapse, and died in battle at Gwalior.

Freedom fighters & leaders

Mangal Pandey

A sepoy of the 34th Bengal Native Infantry who attacked his officers at Barrackpore on 29 March 1857; his execution helped spark the rebellion.

Freedom fighters & leaders

Mahatma Gandhi

Mohandas Gandhi (1869–1948) developed satyagraha (non-violent resistance) in South Africa and led India's freedom movement through the Non-Cooperation, Salt March and Quit India campaigns. He was assassinated in 1948.

Freedom fighters & leaders

Jawaharlal Nehru

India's first Prime Minister (1947–1964), he built democratic institutions, planned industry, scientific institutes, and led the Non-Aligned Movement.

Freedom fighters & leaders

B. R. Ambedkar

A Dalit scholar (1891–1956) with doctorates from Columbia and LSE, he chaired the Constituent Assembly's drafting committee and fought caste discrimination.

Freedom fighters & leaders

Subhas Chandra Bose

A Congress president (1938–39) who led the Indian National Army with Japanese support to fight British rule.

Freedom fighters & leaders

Bhagat Singh

A revolutionary (1907–1931) hanged at 23 for shooting a British officer; his writings argued for socialism.

Freedom fighters & leaders

Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel

India's first Home Minister, he integrated over 560 princely states into India.

Freedom fighters & leaders

Sarojini Naidu

Freedom fighters & leaders

Birsa Munda

A tribal leader (1875–1900) who led the Ulgulan against British and landlords.

Freedom fighters & leaders

Nelson Mandela

Anti-apartheid leader imprisoned for 27 years who became South Africa's first black president (1994).

Civil rights leader who used non-violent protest inspired by Gandhi.

Thinkers, scientists & artists

Confucius

Kong Fuzi (551–479 BCE) taught ethics, family loyalty, ritual and good government; his sayings were compiled as the Analects and became China's state philosophy under the Han.

Thinkers, scientists & artists

Laozi

Traditional author of the Daodejing, founder of Taoism, teaching harmony with the Dao and non-action (wu wei).

Thinkers, scientists & artists

Gautama Buddha

Siddhartha Gautama, a Shakya prince, left home to seek an end to suffering, attained enlightenment at Bodh Gaya, and taught the Four Noble Truths and the Eightfold Path for 45 years.

Thinkers, scientists & artists

Mahavira

The 24th Jain tirthankara taught non-violence (ahimsa), truthfulness and non-attachment.

Thinkers, scientists & artists

Socrates

Athenian philosopher who questioned everything; executed in 399 BCE.

Thinkers, scientists & artists

Thinkers, scientists & artists

Thinkers, scientists & artists

Panini

Thinkers, scientists & artists

Rulers & conquerors

Sargon of Akkad

Born, legend says, to a priestess who set him adrift in a reed basket, Sargon rose from cupbearer to king of Kish and conquered the Sumerian cities, uniting Mesopotamia from the Gulf to Syria. He installed his daughter Enheduanna as high priestess at Ur; she is the earliest author known by name.

Thinkers, scientists & artists

Enheduanna

Daughter of Sargon, she served as high priestess of the moon god Nanna at Ur and composed hymns to Inanna, including the Exaltation of Inanna, which were copied for centuries.

Thinkers, scientists & artists

Imhotep

Chancellor to King Djoser, he designed the Step Pyramid at Saqqara, the first large stone building; later Egyptians worshipped him as a god of medicine.

Rulers & conquerors

Akhenaten

He replaced Egypt's gods with the Aten, built a new capital at Amarna and encouraged a naturalistic art style; after his death his reforms were erased, and his son Tutankhamun restored the old gods.

Thinkers, scientists & artists

Zoroaster

He taught that the world is a struggle between Ahura Mazda (good) and Angra Mainyu (evil), and that humans choose through good thoughts, words and deeds; his hymns (Gathas) are in the Avesta.

Rulers & conquerors

Bimbisara

He expanded Magadha through marriage and conquest and was a patron of the Buddha.

Rulers & conquerors

Pericles

He built the Parthenon and championed democracy; his funeral oration praised Athenian freedom. He died in the plague of Athens.

Thinkers, scientists & artists

Herodotus

His Histories investigated the Persian Wars and the customs of Egyptians, Scythians and Persians, gathering evidence by travel and interviews.

Thinkers, scientists & artists

Hippocrates

He taught that diseases have natural causes; the Hippocratic Oath bears his name.

Thinkers, scientists & artists

Sun Tzu

His treatise on strategy, deception and knowing your enemy is still studied by militaries and businesses.

Rulers & conquerors

Hannibal

He invaded Italy in 218 BCE and destroyed Roman armies at Cannae (216 BCE), but Rome survived; he was defeated by Scipio at Zama (202 BCE).

Thinkers, scientists & artists

Euclid

His Elements organised geometry from axioms and proofs and was used as a textbook for over 2,000 years.

Thinkers, scientists & artists

Sima Qian

He wrote China's first comprehensive history, covering 2,000 years, after enduring castration as punishment rather than suicide.

Thinkers, scientists & artists

Jesus of Nazareth

A Jewish preacher who taught love of God and neighbour and the coming Kingdom of God, he was crucified under Pontius Pilate; his followers proclaimed his resurrection.

Thinkers, scientists & artists

Prophet Muhammad

Muslims believe the Quran was revealed to him from 610; he migrated to Medina (622) and united Arabia.

Rulers & conquerors

Empress Wu Zetian

She ruled as emperor of her own Zhou dynasty (690–705), promoting examinations and Buddhism.
