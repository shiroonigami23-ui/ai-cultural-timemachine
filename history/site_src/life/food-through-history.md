## Hunter-gatherers

Ice Age people ate a wide range of foods: mammoth, reindeer, horse, fish, shellfish, birds, eggs, nuts, roots, berries and wild grains. Starch grains on 30,000-year-old grinding stones show they made a kind of flour. **Cooking** with fire, possibly for a million years, made food easier to digest.

## The first farmers' food

Early farmers ate **porridge and flatbread** made from wheat, barley, millet or rice; **pulses**; and dairy once animals were milked (traces of milk fats appear in pots ~9,000 years old). **Beer** appears in the Near East by ~5,000 years ago and possibly much earlier; **wine** in Georgia by ~6000 BCE.

## Ancient cuisines

:::collapse !From Egypt to the Indus to Rome
- **Egypt:** bread and beer were wages; onions, garlic, lentils, figs, dates, fish; feasts of goose and beef for the rich.
- **Indus:** wheat and barley breads, millets, lentils, peas, sesame oil, dairy, beef and mutton; scientists have found turmeric and ginger residues in Harappan pots at Farmana.
- **Vedic India:** barley cakes, milk, curd, ghee, honey; later rice dishes such as *payasa* (kheer).
- **China:** millet and rice, soybeans, pork; soy sauce and tofu later; tea from the Han era onward.
- **Rome:** wheat porridge, bread, olive oil, wine, *garum* fish sauce; pepper from Kerala.
- **Maya and Aztec:** maize tortillas and tamales (with nixtamalisation, soaking maize in lime), beans, squash, chillies, turkey and **chocolate drinks**.
- **Inca:** potatoes (freeze-dried as *chuño*), quinoa, maize, llama meat, guinea pig.
:::

## India's food story

Indian cuisine grew in layers: native millets, rice, pulses, dairy and spices (**black pepper, cardamom, turmeric, ginger, long pepper**); Persian and Central Asian influences under the Sultanate and Mughals (**biryani, pulao, kebabs, naan, kormas, sherbets**); Portuguese-brought American crops (**chilli, potato, tomato, maize, cashew, papaya, pineapple**, groundnut); and British-era tea drinking, which became mass culture only in the 20th century. Traditional texts such as the *Manasollasa* (12th century, Chalukya king Someshvara III) describe recipes for dosa-like dishes, rice preparations and sweets.

## Famine and plenty

For most of history, harvest failure meant **famine**: the Great Famine of Europe (1315–17), Bengal 1770, Ireland 1845–52, India 1876–78, China 1959–61. Today the world produces enough calories for everyone; about 700 million people still go hungry because of poverty and conflict, while more than a billion are obese.
