History often tells of kings and battles. This page is about everybody else. Each era page in the [Eras catalog](/wiki/eras/index.html) also describes "How people lived".

| Era | Home | Work | Clothes | Life expectancy at birth |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ice Age hunter-gatherer (~20,000 years ago) | Caves, hide tents, mammoth-bone huts | Hunting, gathering, toolmaking | Sewn furs and hides | ~30 (many children died; adults could reach 60+) |
| Neolithic farmer (~7000 BCE) | Mud-brick or wattle houses | Farming, herding, pottery, weaving | Wool, flax | ~25–30 |
| Harappan city dweller (~2300 BCE) | Brick house with a bathroom | Farming, crafts, trade | Cotton | ~30 |
| Roman citizen (~100 CE) | Rented flat (insula) or villa | Trades, farming, army | Wool tunic, toga | ~25–30 |
| Medieval European peasant (~1300) | One-room cottage shared with animals | Field work for the lord | Wool, linen | ~30 |
| Mughal Agra (~1600) | Mud or brick house; havelis for the rich | Crafts, trade, farming, court service | Cotton, silk for elites | ~25–30 |
| Industrial Manchester (~1840) | Back-to-back slum housing | 12–16 hour factory shifts | Cheap cotton | ~25–30 in slums |
| Today (2025) | Apartments, houses | Services, industry, farming | Synthetics, cotton | ~73 (world), ~72 (India) |

Low life expectancy mostly reflects very high **child mortality**: in most pre-modern societies about **a quarter of babies died in their first year** and nearly half of children before age 15 (Our World in Data).

## Childhood

Children worked from an early age: herding, carrying water, spinning. Toys appear in every era: Harappan clay carts and whistles, Egyptian dolls, Roman marbles. Schooling was rare: Indian *gurukuls* and *pathshalas*, Buddhist monasteries and *maktabs*/*madrasas* taught some boys; girls' education was rarer until the 19th–20th centuries (Savitribai Phule opened a girls' school in Pune in 1848).

## Fun and festivals

People played board games (the Royal Game of Ur; India's **chaturanga**, ancestor of chess; **pachisi**, which became Ludo; snakes and ladders, from *moksha patam*), told stories, sang and danced, wrestled and raced. [Festivals](/wiki/festivals/index.html) marked harvests and seasons.

## Health

Without germ theory, infections were the big killers: diarrhoeal diseases, malaria, tuberculosis, smallpox and plague. Yet ancient medicine had real successes: Sushruta's surgery, Egyptian bone-setting, Chinese and Indian variolation against smallpox.
