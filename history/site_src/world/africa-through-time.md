## Life across the ages

:::collapse !Herders, farmers, kingdoms and cities
- **Pastoralists** in the green Sahara herded cattle ~8,000 years ago; as it dried, many moved to the Nile, helping create Egyptian civilisation.
- **Farming** developed independently: sorghum, pearl millet, African rice, yams, teff, coffee and enset.
- **Kingdoms** grew on trade: gold and salt across the Sahara, ivory and gold from the Swahili coast to Arabia and India.
- **Cities**: Meroë's iron furnaces, Aksum's stelae, Timbuktu's manuscript libraries, Kilwa's coral mosques, Great Zimbabwe's stone walls, Benin City's earthworks.
- **Oral history**: griots preserved royal genealogies and epics such as the *Sundiata*.
:::

## Colonial rupture

The slave trade drained West and Central Africa of young people for three centuries. After 1880 European powers seized almost the whole continent; only **Ethiopia** and **Liberia** stayed independent. Colonial borders, forced labour (the Congo Free State killed millions) and extraction shaped modern states. Since independence, Africa has the world's youngest and fastest-growing population.
