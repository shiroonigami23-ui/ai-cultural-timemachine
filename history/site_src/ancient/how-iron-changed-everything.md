---
Copper and bronze were the first metals, but **tin**, needed for bronze, was rare and traded over thousands of kilometres. **Iron ore is everywhere.** Once people learned to smelt it, metal became cheap, and that changed who could farm, fight and rule.

## Learning to smelt iron

:::collapse !From sky metal to steel
- **Meteorite iron** was hammered into beads in Egypt by ~3200 BCE (Tutankhamun's dagger, ~1350 BCE, is meteoritic).
- **Smelting** needs about 1,200 °C and a reducing charcoal fire in a **bloomery** furnace, producing a spongy "bloom" hammered to drive out slag. The **Hittites** of Anatolia worked iron by the 2nd millennium BCE.
- After the **Bronze Age collapse** (~1200 BCE) broke tin trade routes, iron spread rapidly across the Near East, Greece and Europe.
- **India** developed iron independently or very early: iron objects in the Ganga valley and at sites like Malhar date to ~1800–1500 BCE.
- **Africa** smelted iron by ~800–500 BCE (Nok culture, Meroë in Kush), and Bantu-speaking farmers carried it across the continent.
- **China** invented **cast iron** with blast furnaces by the 5th century BCE, a thousand years ahead of Europe.
- **South India and Sri Lanka** made **wootz crucible steel** from ~300 BCE, prized as Damascus steel. Delhi's **Iron Pillar** (~400 CE) has not rusted in 1,600 years.
:::

## What iron changed

1. **Farming:** iron axes cleared dense forests and iron ploughshares broke heavy soils. In India, iron tools helped clear the middle **Ganga plain**, producing the surplus that fed the **mahajanapadas** and the "second urbanisation" of the 6th century BCE.
2. **War:** cheap iron swords, spearheads and armour let kings equip large infantry armies of ordinary men, not just chariot elites. The **Assyrians** built the first great iron-armed empire.
3. **States and empires:** larger surpluses and armies supported bigger states: Assyria, Persia, the Mauryas, Qin China, Rome.
4. **Ideas:** the new wealth, cities and wars of the Iron Age coincided with the **Axial Age** philosophies of the Buddha, Mahavira, Confucius and the Greek thinkers. See [The Axial Age](/wiki/ancient/axial-age.html).
5. **Africa:** iron hoes powered the Bantu expansion, spreading farming and Bantu languages across central and southern Africa.

## From the Iron Age to the industrial age

Iron remained central: Chinese blast furnaces, Indian wootz, European water-powered bellows, then **Abraham Darby's coke smelting (1709)**, **Bessemer steel (1856)** and the **Tata steel plant at Jamshedpur (1907)**. Today the world makes about **1.9 billion tonnes** of steel a year.
