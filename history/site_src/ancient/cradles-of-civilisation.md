A **civilisation**, in historians' terms, usually means a society with **cities, specialised jobs, social classes, monumental buildings, long-distance trade and (usually) writing**. These arose independently in at least six "cradles", most of them on great rivers whose floods renewed the soil.

| Cradle | Earliest cities | River | Writing |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mesopotamia (Sumer) | ~3500 BCE (Uruk) | Tigris, Euphrates | Cuneiform, ~3200 BCE |
| Egypt | ~3100 BCE | Nile | Hieroglyphs, ~3200 BCE |
| Indus (Harappan) | ~2600 BCE (mature phase) | Indus, Ghaggar-Hakra | Indus script (undeciphered) |
| China | ~1600 BCE (Shang) | Yellow River | Oracle-bone script, ~1250 BCE |
| Norte Chico (Peru) | ~3000 BCE (Caral) | Supe valley | None (later quipu) |
| Mesoamerica | ~1200 BCE (Olmec) | Gulf lowlands | Maya script, ~300 BCE |

## Mesopotamia: land between the rivers

**Sumerian** city-states such as **Uruk** (perhaps 40,000+ people by 3000 BCE) and **Ur** were ruled by priest-kings from temples on top of stepped **ziggurats**. Scribes invented **cuneiform** to record grain and beer rations. People ate barley bread and drank beer through straws, worshipped city gods, and told the **Epic of Gilgamesh**, with its flood story. **Hammurabi's** code (~1754 BCE) set out 282 laws ("an eye for an eye", but punishments depended on class).

## Egypt: gift of the Nile

Egypt was unified around **3100 BCE**. Its year had three seasons set by the river: **Akhet** (flood), **Peret** (growing) and **Shemu** (harvest). Farmers paid taxes in grain and worked on royal projects such as the **Great Pyramid** (~2560 BCE), built by paid, organised labour crews, not enslaved people as once thought. Egyptians ate bread, onions, lentils, fish and beer; the rich ate beef, wine and honey. Belief in the afterlife led to mummification and the *Book of the Dead*.

## China: the Shang and the Mandate of Heaven

The **Shang** kings at Anyang asked their ancestors questions by heating **oracle bones** and reading the cracks, inscribing them with the earliest Chinese characters. The **Zhou** (from 1046 BCE) claimed the **Mandate of Heaven**: rulers who governed badly lost the right to rule. Millet was the staple in the north and rice in the south.

## The Americas

**Caral** in Peru built pyramids around the same time as Egypt's, trading cotton and anchovies. In Mesoamerica the **Olmec** carved colossal stone heads; their successors, the **Maya**, invented a complete writing system, a calendar and a zero.
