title: Rome: Rise, Conquest and Fall
summary: How a small Italian city conquered the Mediterranean, Gaul and Britain; how ordinary Romans lived and ate; and why the Western Empire fell in 476 CE.

---
According to legend Rome was founded in **753 BCE** by Romulus. It became a **republic** in 509 BCE, ruled by elected **consuls** and the **Senate**, and over five centuries conquered Italy and then the whole Mediterranean.

## Conquests

:::collapse !From city to empire
- **Italy** (to 264 BCE), through alliances and colonies linked by roads like the **Via Appia**.
- **Punic Wars** (264–146 BCE) against **Carthage**, including Hannibal's crossing of the Alps with elephants (218 BCE) and his crushing victory at **Cannae**. Rome destroyed Carthage in 146 BCE.
- **Greece and the East**: Macedonia, Greece (146 BCE), Asia Minor, Syria, Judea.
- **Gaul**: **Julius Caesar** conquered it in 58–50 BCE, defeating **Vercingetorix** at Alesia; perhaps a million Gauls died and a million were enslaved, by ancient estimates.
- **Egypt** (30 BCE) after Cleopatra's death.
- **Britain** (from 43 CE under Claudius); **Boudica's** revolt (60–61 CE) burned London before being crushed; **Hadrian's Wall** (122 CE) marked the northern frontier.
- **Dacia** (Romania) under Trajan; the empire peaked in **117 CE**, with perhaps 60–70 million people.
:::

After civil wars, Caesar's heir **Octavian** became **Augustus**, the first emperor (27 BCE), beginning two centuries of relative peace, the **Pax Romana**.

## Life in Rome

- **Homes:** the rich lived in *domus* houses with courtyards; most city dwellers rented cramped flats in multi-storey *insulae* that often burned or collapsed.
- **Food:** the poor ate wheat porridge (*puls*), bread, beans, vegetables and cheap wine; Rome gave free grain (later bread) to about 200,000 citizens. The rich held banquets with dormice, oysters and peacock, flavoured with ***garum*** (fermented fish sauce) and pepper imported from **India's Malabar coast**.
- **Work and slavery:** perhaps 10–20% of the population of Italy was enslaved.
- **Entertainment:** chariot racing at the Circus Maximus (seating ~150,000), gladiators at the **Colosseum**, public **baths**.
- **Pompeii and Herculaneum**, buried by **Vesuvius in 79 CE**, preserve shops, bakeries, graffiti and food remains.

## Rome and India

Roman ships sailed with the monsoon winds from Egypt to **Muziris** (Kerala) and **Arikamedu** (Puducherry), trading gold coins, wine and glass for **pepper, pearls, cotton, gems and ivory**. Hoards of Roman coins are found across South India, and the writer Pliny complained that India drained Rome of 50 million sesterces a year.

## Why the West fell

The **Western Empire** ended in **476 CE** when the Germanic general **Odoacer** deposed Romulus Augustulus. Causes, debated for centuries since Edward Gibbon, include civil wars and coups, economic and currency crises, the **Antonine** and **Cyprian plagues**, climate cooling, pressure from **Huns** and Germanic peoples, and the split into East and West. The **Eastern (Byzantine) Empire** lasted until **1453**.
