The **Indus Valley** or **Harappan Civilisation** was the largest of the Bronze Age civilisations by area, spanning about **1 million km²** of today's Pakistan and northwest India. Its mature urban phase lasted from about **2600 to 1900 BCE**.

:::facts At a glance
- Discovered: **1920–21**, at Harappa (Daya Ram Sahni) and Mohenjo-daro (R. D. Banerji)
- Major cities: **Harappa, Mohenjo-daro, Ganweriwala** (Pakistan); **Rakhigarhi, Dholavira, Lothal, Kalibangan, Banawali** (India)
- More than **1,000** sites known, over half in India, many along the dried-up **Ghaggar–Hakra** river
- Population: perhaps **1–5 million**
- Script: about 400 signs on 4,000+ objects, mostly short seal inscriptions; **still undeciphered**
:::

## Cities built to a plan

Streets ran in a rough **grid**. Houses of **standard-ratio baked bricks** (1:2:4) had private wells, bathing platforms and toilets that drained into **covered street drains** with inspection holes, sanitation not matched in many cities until the 19th century. Mohenjo-daro's **Great Bath** may have been used for ritual bathing. **Dholavira** in Kutch had huge stone-cut reservoirs to store monsoon water, and a ten-sign "signboard". **Lothal** in Gujarat has a large basin often interpreted as a dockyard.

## Daily life and food

Most people were **farmers and herders**. They grew **wheat, barley, peas, lentils, chickpeas, sesame, mustard and cotton** (among the earliest cotton anywhere), and, in Gujarat and the east, **millets and some rice**. They kept humped **zebu cattle**, buffalo, sheep and goats, and ate fish. Residue in pots from Haryana sites shows cooking of meat and dairy. Craftspeople made **carnelian beads** (some drilled with remarkable stone drills), shell bangles, bronze tools, terracotta toys on wheels and the famous **"Dancing Girl"** bronze. Trade reached **Mesopotamia**, where "Meluhha" (probably the Indus) supplied carnelian, timber and ivory.

There is little sign of grand palaces, royal tombs or big armies, which has led some scholars to suggest an unusually **egalitarian** or merchant-led society, though this is debated.

## Who were the Harappans?

The genome of a woman from **Rakhigarhi** (Shinde et al. 2019) showed a mix of ancient South Asian hunter-gatherer ancestry and an Iranian-related ancestry that separated before farming, with **no steppe ancestry**. Most South Asians today descend partly from Indus-related people (Narasimhan et al. 2019).

## Decline

From about **1900 BCE** the big cities emptied and people moved east and south into smaller villages. Explanations include:
- a **weakening monsoon** around the **4.2-kiloyear event**, shown in lake and cave records (Giosan et al. 2012; Sarkar et al. 2016);
- rivers such as the Ghaggar–Hakra **drying up or shifting**;
- **declining trade** with Mesopotamia.

Farmers adapted by switching to **drought-tolerant millets and summer crops**. It was not a sudden collapse but a transformation, and many Harappan traditions (weights, bangles, possibly some deities and yoga-like postures on seals) may have continued into later Indian culture.
