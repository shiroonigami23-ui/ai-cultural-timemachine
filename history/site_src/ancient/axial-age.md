German philosopher **Karl Jaspers** named the period **800–200 BCE** the *Achsenzeit*, or **Axial Age**, because so many of humanity's lasting religious and philosophical traditions took shape in it, in separate places, at about the same time.

| Region | Thinkers and traditions |
|---|---|
| India | The **Upanishads** (atman, brahman, karma, moksha); **Gautama Buddha**; **Mahavira** and Jainism; materialist **Charvaka**; grammarian **Panini** |
| China | **Confucius** (ethics and good government), **Laozi** (Taoism), **Mozi** (universal love), **Mencius**, legalists |
| Persia | **Zoroaster**'s teachings on good and evil (dates debated) |
| Israel | The Hebrew prophets **Isaiah, Jeremiah** |
| Greece | **Thales, Pythagoras, Heraclitus, Socrates, Plato, Aristotle** |

## Why then?

Suggested reasons include: **iron tools** and bigger harvests that fed cities and states; **coinage and trade** that created a wealthy, mobile, literate class; **wars between states** that made people question tradition; and new **urban anxieties** about suffering and meaning. In India, the **second urbanisation** of the Ganga plain, with its 16 *mahajanapadas*, provided the setting for the Buddha and Mahavira, who taught in Magadha and Kosala.

## Common threads

Many Axial teachers turned away from sacrifice and ritual toward **inner transformation, ethics and compassion**: *ahimsa* (non-violence) in Jainism and Buddhism, the Golden Rule in Confucius, the examined life for Socrates. Some historians (such as Ian Morris) link this to rising prosperity; others doubt whether the "age" was really unified.
