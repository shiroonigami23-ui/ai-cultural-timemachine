---
title: The Late Bronze Age Collapse
summary: Around 1200 BCE the interconnected world of the eastern Mediterranean fell apart within decades. What happened, the suspects, and what grew from the ruins.
level: Intermediate
---
Around **1200–1150 BCE** a whole system of kingdoms around the eastern Mediterranean collapsed. The **Mycenaean** palaces of Greece burned, the **Hittite** Empire vanished, the trading city of **Ugarit** was destroyed, and Egypt survived only after desperate battles. Writing disappeared from Greece for about 400 years (a "Dark Age").

## The world before the fall

The Late Bronze Age was the first truly **globalised** era: Egypt, the Hittites, Babylon, Assyria and Mycenae exchanged letters (the **Amarna letters**), royal gifts and princesses. Ships like the **Uluburun** wreck (~1300 BCE) carried copper from Cyprus, tin from Central Asia, glass, ivory and ebony. Bronze needed both copper and tin, so every kingdom depended on trade.

## The suspects

:::collapse !A "perfect storm"
- **Drought:** pollen and lake records show a 150-year dry spell; letters from Ugarit beg for grain.
- **Earthquakes:** a possible "earthquake storm" in the Aegean and Levant.
- **The Sea Peoples:** raiders and migrants recorded by Ramesses III at Medinet Habu (~1177 BCE).
- **Systems collapse:** when trade broke down, palace economies that depended on it failed one after another.
- **Internal revolt** and new warfare (infantry with long swords against chariots).
:::

Historian Eric Cline argues that no single cause was enough, but together they toppled an interdependent system, a lesson often applied to our own globalised world.

## What came after

From the ruins came the **Iron Age** (iron ore is common, so metal no longer depended on long tin routes), the **Phoenician alphabet**, the kingdoms of **Israel and Judah**, the Neo-Assyrian Empire, and eventually Classical Greece.
