## How people lived in each age

:::collapse !Stone Age and Indus
Hunter-gatherers lived in rock shelters such as Bhimbetka, eating game, fish, honey and wild plants. Indus city dwellers lived in brick houses with bathrooms; they ate wheat and barley flatbreads, pulses, dairy and meat, and wore cotton.
:::

:::collapse !Vedic and Mahajanapada age
Cattle were the main measure of wealth; people ate barley cakes, milk, ghee, curd and, in early times, meat, while rice spread in the east. Villages (*grama*) grouped into clans and then kingdoms. Iron ploughs cleared the Ganga forests.
:::

:::collapse !Mauryan and Gupta India
Megasthenes, a Greek ambassador, described Pataliputra's 9-mile wooden walls, guilds, markets and seven social groups. Most people farmed rice, wheat, barley, millets and pulses, with sugarcane and cotton as cash crops. Gupta-era towns were prosperous; Chinese pilgrim Faxian reported rest houses and free hospitals.
:::

:::collapse !Early medieval and the south
Temple towns (Thanjavur, Kanchipuram, Madurai) became centres of land, crafts, music and dance. Chola inscriptions at **Uttaramerur** describe village assemblies elected by lottery (*kudavolai*). The south ate rice, millets, coconut, pepper and tamarind; the north wheat, barley and pulses.
:::

:::collapse !Sultanate and Mughal India
Persian, Turkic and Indian cultures blended in music (khayal, qawwali), language (Urdu), architecture and cuisine (biryani, kebabs, naan, Mughlai gravies). Crops from the Americas, including **chilli, potato, tomato, maize, tobacco, cashew and pineapple**, arrived through the Portuguese and transformed Indian cooking. Around 1700, India produced roughly a quarter of world output (Maddison), and Bengal's muslin and Gujarat's cottons clothed the world.
:::

:::collapse !Colonial India
Railways and telegraphs were built mainly to move raw materials and troops. Handloom weaving collapsed under British mill cloth; **famines** killed tens of millions; per-capita income stagnated. Social reformers (Ram Mohan Roy, Savitribai and Jyotirao Phule, Vidyasagar, Ambedkar) challenged sati, caste and illiteracy. See [The East India Company and the Raj](/wiki/india/east-india-company-and-the-raj.html).
:::
