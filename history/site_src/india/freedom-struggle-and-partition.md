## Early nationalism (1885–1915)

The **Indian National Congress** met first in Bombay in 1885. Its "moderates" (Dadabhai Naoroji, Gopal Krishna Gokhale) petitioned for reforms and documented the **drain of wealth**. The **"extremists"**, Bal Gangadhar Tilak ("Swaraj is my birthright"), Lala Lajpat Rai and Bipin Chandra Pal, demanded self-rule. The **1905 Partition of Bengal** sparked the **Swadeshi movement**, a boycott of British goods. The **Muslim League** was founded in 1906.

## The Gandhian era (1915–1947)

:::collapse !Mass movements
- **Champaran (1917)** for indigo farmers and **Kheda (1918)**: Gandhi's first Indian satyagrahas.
- **Jallianwala Bagh (13 April 1919):** troops under General Dyer fired on a peaceful crowd in Amritsar, killing hundreds.
- **Non-Cooperation (1920–22):** boycotts of courts, schools and cloth; called off after violence at Chauri Chaura.
- **Salt March (1930):** Gandhi walked 390 km to **Dandi** to make salt, launching **civil disobedience**; tens of thousands were jailed.
- **Quit India (August 1942):** "Do or die"; leaders were arrested and the movement went underground.
:::

Other currents ran alongside: revolutionaries such as **Bhagat Singh**, **Chandrashekhar Azad** and **Ram Prasad Bismil**; **Subhas Chandra Bose's Indian National Army**, whose 1945–46 trials stirred the country and helped trigger the **1946 naval mutiny**; tribal rebellions (**Birsa Munda's Ulgulan**, the **Santhal Hul**); peasant and labour movements; and **B. R. Ambedkar's** struggle against caste.

## Independence and Partition

Exhausted by the Second World War, Britain agreed to leave. On **15 August 1947**, India became independent and **Pakistan** was created from the Muslim-majority regions of the northwest and Bengal. The border, drawn in five weeks by **Cyril Radcliffe**, who had never visited India, was published two days after independence.

About **15 million people** crossed the new borders, and **between 200,000 and 2 million** were killed in riots and massacres, with mass abductions and assaults on women. Punjab and Bengal were split. The princely states were integrated by **Sardar Patel** and **V. P. Menon**; the disputes over **Kashmir** and **Hyderabad** led to conflict.

On **26 January 1950** India adopted its **Constitution**, drafted under **Ambedkar**, guaranteeing universal adult franchise from the first general election (1951–52).
