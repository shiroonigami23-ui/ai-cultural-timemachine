sumer|Most Sumerians were farmers and herders working temple and palace lands, paid in barley rations. Cities held craftsmen, merchants, scribes and priests; children of the elite trained for years in scribal schools (edubba). Homes were mud-brick houses around a courtyard.|Each city had a patron god (Inanna at Uruk, Nanna at Ur) living in the temple. Festivals such as the New Year Akitu, epic poetry, lyres from the Royal Tombs of Ur and cylinder seals show a rich culture.
akkadian-empire|Farmers and soldiers supplied a standing army; Akkadian became the language of administration alongside Sumerian.|Sargon's daughter Enheduanna, high priestess of Ur, is the first named author in history.
babylonia|Babylon was a cosmopolitan city of perhaps 200,000 under Nebuchadnezzar, with merchants, astronomer-priests and canal farmers.|The Akitu festival, the Enuma Elish creation epic and records of planetary motion kept for centuries.
assyria|A militarised society where conscripted farmers fought seasonal campaigns and conquered peoples were resettled across the empire.|Lion-hunt reliefs, winged bull guardians (lamassu) and the Library of Ashurbanipal.
ancient-egypt|Farmers' lives followed the Nile's three seasons; during the flood they worked on royal projects for pay. Scribes, priests and artisans lived in towns; workers' villages like Deir el-Medina left letters, receipts and even records of strikes.|Gods such as Ra, Osiris, Isis and Hathor; festivals like Opet; mummification and the Book of the Dead; painted tombs, music, board games (senet) and cosmetics.
hittite-empire|Farmers, chariot warriors and scribes in a kingdom bound by treaties with vassals.|The 'thousand gods' of Hatti, laws milder than Babylon's, and royal archives at Hattusa.
phoenicia|Sailors, merchants, dyers and shipbuilders lived in crowded island and harbour cities.|Gods Baal and Astarte; ivory carving, glassware and the alphabet carried across the Mediterranean.
ancient-israel-and-judah|Most people farmed hill terraces growing grain, olives and grapes; life centred on clans and village elders.|Sabbath, Passover and Temple worship; the Hebrew Bible's law, prophecy and poetry.
kingdom-of-kush|Farmers along the Nile, herders and ironworkers; powerful queen mothers (kandakes) shaped politics.|The lion god Apedemak, Nubian pyramids and Meroitic script.
carthage|A merchant and maritime people with large farming estates in Tunisia.|Gods Baal Hammon and Tanit; famous agriculture treatise by Mago.
achaemenid-persian-empire|Farmers paid tribute; craftsmen from across the empire built Persepolis for wages recorded on tablets.|Zoroastrian fire temples, the Nowruz new year, and audience reliefs of 23 nations bringing gifts.
aksum|Farmers grew teff and barley; merchants traded ivory and gold to Rome and India.|Early Christianity, giant stelae and the Ge'ez script still used in Ethiopian liturgy.
ghana-empire|Soninke farmers and traders controlled gold coming from the south and salt from the Sahara.|Royal courts with elaborate ceremonies described by Arab geographers.
mali-empire|Farmers, herders, fishers and traders lived under clan-based social orders; griots preserved history in song.|The Sundiata epic, Timbuktu's Sankore university and manuscripts, mud mosques renewed each year.
songhai-empire|River fishermen, farmers and merchants along the Niger.|Islamic scholarship in Timbuktu and Gao.
great-zimbabwe|Cattle herders and farmers; an elite lived inside the stone enclosures.|Soapstone bird carvings now on Zimbabwe's flag.
swahili-city-states|Merchants, sailors and craftsmen in coral-stone towns.|Swahili poetry, Islamic architecture and Indian Ocean dress and food.
kingdom-of-benin|Guild craftsmen, farmers and court officials lived in a planned walled city.|Bronze plaques recording court history; the Igue festival.
ottoman-empire|Farmers under the timar system, urban guilds, merchants and a multi-faith population organised in millets.|Coffee houses, Iznik tiles, Sinan's mosques, calligraphy, music and the Turkish bath.
safavid-iran|Silk farmers, carpet weavers and merchants; Isfahan held perhaps half a million people.|Miniature painting, carpets, Shia festivals such as Muharram.
indus-valley-civilisation|Most people farmed or herded; city dwellers worked as potters, bead-makers, metalworkers and traders. Brick houses had wells and bathrooms; children played with clay carts and whistles.|Seals with animals and the 'unicorn', terracotta figurines, the Dancing Girl bronze, and possible fire altars at Kalibangan.
vedic-india|Clans of herders and farmers measured wealth in cattle; later, settled villages grew barley and rice.|Hymns of the Rigveda, fire sacrifices (yajna), gods such as Indra, Agni and Varuna, and the oral tradition that preserved the Vedas.
magadha|Rice farmers, ironworkers and traders in a fertile, rising kingdom.|Home ground of the Buddha's and Mahavira's teaching; early stupas and viharas.
maurya-empire|Farmers paid around a quarter of their harvest in tax; cities had guilds, markets and wooden mansions. Megasthenes described seven social groups and well-run cities.|Ashoka's dhamma, Buddhist missions to Sri Lanka and beyond, polished stone pillars and the Sanchi stupa.
satavahana-dynasty|Deccan farmers and merchants trading with Rome; Buddhist monasteries supported by traders.|Amaravati sculpture and Karla caves.
sangam-era-tamilakam|People lived by five landscapes (tinai): hills, forests, farmland, coast and desert, each with its own work and gods.|Sangam love and war poetry (akam and puram), hero stones, Murugan worship.
kushan-empire|A mix of nomad elites, farmers and Silk Road merchants.|Gandhara and Mathura art made the first Buddha images in human form.
gupta-empire|Prosperous farmers and guilds; Faxian described peaceful towns and free hospitals.|Kalidasa's drama, Ajanta murals, temple building, Sanskrit learning and the Puranas.
pallava-dynasty|Farmers in the Kaveri and Palar basins; port trade from Mamallapuram.|Rock-cut temples and the bhakti poetry of the Alvars and Nayanars.
chalukya-dynasty|Farmers and traders of the Deccan plateau.|Temple experiments mixing northern and southern styles at Pattadakal.
rashtrakuta-dynasty|An agrarian empire of the Deccan with powerful feudatories.|Kannada literature and the Ellora Kailasa temple.
chola-empire|Rice farmers under elected village assemblies; temple towns employed dancers, musicians and craftsmen.|Bronze Nataraja, temple festivals, Tamil devotional poetry and Bharatanatyam roots.
pala-empire|Rice farmers and traders of Bengal and Bihar.|Buddhist universities of Nalanda and Vikramashila and Pala sculpture.
delhi-sultanate|Peasant villages paid revenue to iqta holders; cities grew with Persian-speaking elites and Indian artisans.|Sufi shrines, qawwali (Amir Khusrau), Indo-Islamic architecture and the birth of Hindustani.
vijayanagara-empire|Farmers, temple communities and merchants; Hampi's bazaars sold rubies and horses.|Mahanavami festival, Carnatic music and Telugu and Kannada poetry.
ahom-kingdom|Wet-rice farmers organised by the paik labour system.|Buranji chronicles and Bihu festivities.
mughal-empire|Most people were peasants paying a third or more of their harvest; cities held weavers, merchants and nobles' households.|Miniature painting, Persian and Urdu poetry, Hindustani music, gardens, and cuisine such as biryani.
maratha-confederacy|Deccan peasants, soldiers and hill-fort garrisons.|Bhakti saints like Tukaram and festivals such as Ganesh Chaturthi.
sikh-empire|Punjabi farmers and soldiers in a multi-faith state.|Gurdwara langar, Baisakhi and the gilding of the Golden Temple.
kingdom-of-mysore|Farmers and silk weavers; artisans for the court.|Mysore Dasara and Mysore painting.
east-india-company-rule|Peasants paid high land revenue; weavers lost markets to British cloth.|Company paintings and early printing presses in Calcutta.
british-raj|Most Indians remained farmers facing famines; cities grew with mills, railways and an English-educated middle class.|The Bengal Renaissance, new Indian newspapers, and the rise of nationalism.
xia-and-shang-china|Millet farmers, bronze casters and diviners lived around royal centres.|Ancestor worship and oracle-bone divination.
zhou-dynasty|Peasants worked land for lords under a feudal order.|Confucian rites, the Book of Songs and Taoist ideas.
qin-dynasty|Peasants were conscripted for armies and huge building projects.|Standardised writing and legalist philosophy.
han-dynasty|Peasant families farmed with iron ploughs; officials studied Confucian classics.|Silk, lacquer, paper and the historian Sima Qian.
tang-dynasty|Chang'an was a cosmopolitan city of about a million.|Poetry of Li Bai and Du Fu, tea culture and Buddhist art.
song-dynasty|Commercial towns, rice farmers and scholar-officials.|Landscape painting, printed books and the tea house.
yuan-dynasty|Mongol, Chinese and foreign populations under a layered order.|Yuan drama and blue-and-white porcelain.
ming-dynasty|A large farming population and busy market towns.|Porcelain, Ming novels and the Forbidden City.
qing-dynasty|A population boom to about 400 million farmers.|Peking opera and Qing literature.
goguryeo-baekje-and-silla|Farmers and aristocratic elites.|Buddhist temples such as Bulguksa.
joseon-korea|Confucian society of yangban, farmers and artisans.|Hangul, pansori and kimchi culture.
heian-japan|Aristocrats in Kyoto; peasants farmed rice.|The Tale of Genji and waka poetry.
edo-japan-tokugawa|Samurai, farmers, artisans and merchants.|Kabuki, haiku (Bashō) and ukiyo-e prints.
funan|Farmers and port traders.|Hindu and Buddhist worship.
srivijaya|Sailors and traders around Palembang.|Buddhist learning centres.
khmer-empire|Rice farmers supporting temple cities.|Apsara dance and Angkor's reliefs.
majapahit|Farmers and traders across Java.|Wayang shadow puppetry and Old Javanese poetry.
polynesian-voyagers|Island farmers and fishers.|Tattooing, navigation lore and oral genealogies.
minoan-civilisation|Farmers, sailors and palace craftsmen.|Bull-leaping frescoes.
mycenaean-greece|Palace economies of farmers and warriors.|Gold masks and epic oral tradition.
classical-greece|Citizen farmers, artisans and enslaved workers.|Theatre, the Olympics and philosophy.
macedonian-empire|Soldiers and settlers across new cities.|Hellenistic art and libraries.
roman-republic|Farmers and soldiers; growing slavery.|Roman law and festivals like Saturnalia.
roman-empire|City dwellers in insulae, peasants and enslaved workers.|Baths, games, Latin literature.
byzantine-empire|Farmers, monks and urban traders.|Icons, mosaics and chariot races.
celts|Tribal farmers and warriors.|Druidic rituals and La Tène art.
vikings|Farmers, sailors and traders.|Sagas, runes and the thing assembly.
holy-roman-empire|Peasants under lords; free cities of merchants.|Gothic cathedrals and printing.
kievan-rus|Farmers and river traders.|Orthodox icons and onion domes.
spanish-empire|Colonists, Indigenous labourers and enslaved Africans.|Baroque churches and mestizo culture.
portuguese-empire|Sailors, traders and colonists.|Azulejo tiles and Indo-Portuguese culture in Goa.
british-empire|Settlers, colonial subjects and indentured labourers.|Cricket, the English language and global sport.
russian-empire|Serfs, nobles and merchants.|Tolstoy, Tchaikovsky and Orthodox festivals.
soviet-union|Collective farmers and industrial workers.|Constructivist art, ballet and space-age culture.
norte-chico-caral|Fishers and cotton farmers.|Flutes and ceremonial plazas.
olmec|Farmers and ceremonial centres.|Jaguar imagery and the ballgame.
maya-civilisation|Maize farmers in villages around temple cities.|The ballgame, glyph writing and the Popol Vuh.
teotihuacan|Craftsmen and farmers in apartment compounds.|Feathered serpent imagery.
aztec-empire|Calpulli neighbourhoods of farmers and artisans.|Feathered art, poetry and markets like Tlatelolco.
inca-empire|Ayllu kin groups of farmers owing labour (mit'a).|Inti Raymi and fine textiles.
ancestral-puebloans|Farmers in pueblos.|Kivas and pottery.
mississippian-culture|Maize farmers around mound centres.|Shell gorgets and ceremonial mounds.
haudenosaunee-confederacy|Longhouse families led by clan mothers.|The Great Law of Peace and wampum belts.
united-states|Farmers, industrial workers and later a service economy.|Jazz, Hollywood and the internet.
