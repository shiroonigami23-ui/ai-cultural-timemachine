Every human story starts with a strange choice: walking on two legs. No other living primate does it all the time. Fossils show it came **first**, millions of years before big brains, tools or fire.

## A changing Africa

Between about **10 and 5 million years ago**, Africa became cooler and drier as global climate shifted and the East African Rift uplifted. Continuous forests broke into a **mosaic of woodland, gallery forest along rivers and grassland**. Apes living at the forest edges had to cross open ground between patches of trees.

## The fossil steps

:::collapse !From Toumaï to Lucy
- **~7 million years ago: *Sahelanthropus*** (Chad). Its skull opening suggests the head sat upright on the spine, a hint of bipedalism.
- **~6 million years ago: *Orrorin*** (Kenya). Thigh bones built for carrying weight on two legs.
- **4.4 million years ago: *Ardipithecus ramidus*, "Ardi"** (Ethiopia). Walked upright on the ground but still had a grasping big toe for climbing. Ardi lived in **woodland, not savanna**, overturning the old idea that we stood up because we moved onto open plains.
- **3.66 million years ago: Laetoli footprints** (Tanzania). Three individuals walking with a human-like stride, preserved in volcanic ash.
- **3.2 million years ago: "Lucy", *Australopithecus afarensis*** (Ethiopia). A pelvis and knee like ours, but long arms and curved fingers for climbing. Her species lasted nearly a million years.
:::

## Why walk upright? Leading ideas

1. **Energy efficiency:** walking on two legs uses about a quarter of the energy chimpanzees spend knuckle-walking (Sockol et al. 2007).
2. **Carrying:** free hands could carry food, infants and later tools.
3. **Seeing over grass** and **reducing heat**: standing tall exposes less body to the midday sun and more to cooling breezes.
4. **Reaching fruit** from the ground, like orangutans walking along branches.

Most scientists think several of these worked together.

## The costs

Bipedalism came with trade-offs we still live with: **back pain, knee problems, varicose veins** and, above all, **difficult childbirth**, because a narrower walking pelvis meets a growing baby's head. Human babies are born helpless partly because of this, which made **cooperation and long childhoods** essential.

## What it opened up

Free hands led to **stone tools** (3.3 million years ago), **carrying meat and water**, and long-distance **walking and endurance running** in *Homo erectus*. It set the stage for every later step: fire, language, migration across the world.
