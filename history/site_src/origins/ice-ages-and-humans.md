For the last **2.6 million years** (the Quaternary period) Earth has swung between long cold **glacials** and shorter warm **interglacials**. We live in an interglacial, the **Holocene**, which began **11,700 years ago**. All of human evolution happened in this restless climate.

## Why ice ages happen

Serbian scientist **Milutin Milanković** showed that slow changes in Earth's orbit change how much summer sunlight reaches the far north:
- **Eccentricity** (how oval the orbit is), ~100,000-year cycle;
- **Obliquity** (tilt, 22.1°–24.5°), ~41,000-year cycle;
- **Precession** (wobble), ~19,000–23,000-year cycle.

When northern summers are cool, snow survives, ice sheets grow and reflect more sunlight, and CO₂ falls (from ~280 ppm in warm periods to ~180 ppm in glacial ones), deepening the cold. Ice cores from Antarctica record these cycles for 800,000 years.

## The world at the Last Glacial Maximum (~26,000–19,000 years ago)

:::facts A different planet
- Ice sheets up to **3–4 km thick** covered Canada, the northern USA, Scandinavia and Britain
- Sea level was about **120 m lower**: Britain joined Europe (Doggerland), Siberia joined Alaska (Beringia), Sri Lanka joined India, and Australia joined New Guinea (Sahul)
- Global average temperature was about **6 °C colder** than pre-industrial
- Much of the tropics, including India, was **drier**; the Thar expanded and the monsoon weakened
- The Himalayan glaciers extended far lower down the valleys
:::

## The mammoth steppe and its giants

A vast cold grassland, the **mammoth steppe**, stretched from Spain to Canada. It supported **woolly mammoths, woolly rhinos, cave lions, cave bears, giant deer (Irish elk), steppe bison and wild horses**. In the Americas lived giant ground sloths, glyptodonts, sabre-toothed *Smilodon* and dire wolves; in Australia, *Diprotodon* and the giant lizard *Megalania*. In India's Narmada valley, the giant elephant ***Palaeoloxodon namadicus*** roamed. Browse them in [Species of past ages](/wiki/ancient_species/index.html).

Most of these **megafauna** went extinct between about 50,000 and 10,000 years ago. The timing tracks the arrival of modern humans on each continent, combined with rapid climate shifts. The last woolly mammoths survived on Wrangel Island until about 4,000 years ago.

## How Ice Age people lived

People adapted brilliantly: **sewn clothing** using bone needles, **mammoth-bone houses** in Ukraine, spear-throwers and later bows, and **dogs** domesticated from wolves (by at least ~15,000 years ago). They painted the animals they hunted in caves such as **Lascaux** and **Chauvet** (France), **Altamira** (Spain) and in rock shelters such as **Bhimbetka** (India).

## The thaw

From about 19,000 years ago the ice melted, with a sudden cold relapse, the **Younger Dryas** (12,900–11,700 years ago). Seas rose by over 100 m, drowning coasts where many early sites now lie underwater. When the Holocene began, the climate became unusually **warm and stable**, and within a few thousand years people in several regions independently began to farm. See [The Agricultural Revolution](/wiki/origins/agricultural-revolution.html).

## Smaller ice ages in history

- **Late Antique Little Ice Age (536–660 CE):** volcanic eruptions brought a decade of cold summers alongside Justinian's plague (Büntgen et al. 2016).
- **Little Ice Age (~1300–1850):** frost fairs on the frozen Thames, Alpine glaciers advancing, famines in Europe.
