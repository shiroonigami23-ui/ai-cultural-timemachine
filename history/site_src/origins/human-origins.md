Humans are great apes. Our lineage split from the ancestors of chimpanzees roughly **6–7 million years ago** in Africa. For most of the time since, **several human species lived at once**. Only in the last ~40,000 years has *Homo sapiens* been alone.

:::facts Key dates
- ~7–6 million years ago: earliest possible hominins (*Sahelanthropus*, Chad)
- 4.4 million years ago: *Ardipithecus* (Ethiopia), part tree-climber, part walker
- 3.2 million years ago: "Lucy" (*Australopithecus afarensis*) walks upright
- 3.3 million years ago: the oldest stone tools (Lomekwi, Kenya)
- ~1.9 million years ago: *Homo erectus*, with a human body plan, leaves Africa
- ~1.5 million years ago: Acheulean hand-axes at **Attirampakkam**, near Chennai (Pappu et al. 2011)
- ~300,000 years ago: earliest *Homo sapiens* fossils at **Jebel Irhoud**, Morocco (Hublin et al. 2017)
- ~70,000–50,000 years ago: modern humans spread out of Africa
- ~65,000 years ago: people reach Australia (Clarkson et al. 2017)
:::

## A crowded family tree

:::collapse !The human species that shared the planet
- ***Homo habilis*** ("handy man"), ~2.4–1.4 million years ago: larger brain, simple Oldowan tools.
- ***Homo erectus***, ~1.9 million–110,000 years ago: long legs, controlled fire, crossed into Asia (Java Man, Peking Man). Probably the first to travel out of Africa.
- ***Homo heidelbergensis***, ~700,000–300,000 years ago: likely ancestor of Neanderthals, Denisovans and us.
- **Neanderthals** (*Homo neanderthalensis*), ~400,000–40,000 years ago: Europe and western Asia; stocky, cold-adapted, buried their dead, made jewellery and possibly cave art.
- **Denisovans**: known mainly from DNA and a few fossils from Siberia, Tibet and Taiwan; a Denisovan gene variant (*EPAS1*) helps Tibetans live at high altitude.
- ***Homo floresiensis*** ("hobbit"), Flores, Indonesia: about 1 m tall, lived until ~50,000 years ago.
- ***Homo naledi***, South Africa: small-brained yet living ~300,000 years ago.
:::

## What made us human

- **Walking upright** freed the hands and saved energy over long distances.
- **Tools and fire** let early humans eat meat and cooked food, fuelling bigger brains (our brain uses about 20% of our energy).
- **Long childhoods and cooperation**: grandmothers, sharing food, teaching.
- **Language and symbols**: ochre engraved at Blombos Cave, South Africa (~75,000 years ago); shell beads; later cave art.

## Out of Africa, into India

Genetics shows all living non-Africans descend mainly from a group that left Africa around **70,000–50,000 years ago**. They met and interbred with Neanderthals (non-Africans carry about 1–2% Neanderthal DNA) and Denisovans (up to ~4–6% in Papuans and Aboriginal Australians). Moving along the southern coast of Asia, they reached India, Southeast Asia and Australia.

In India, stone tools at **Jwalapuram** (Andhra Pradesh) lie above and below ash from the **Toba super-eruption** of ~74,000 years ago, suggesting people survived it (Petraglia et al. 2007). The **Andamanese** peoples and the hunter-gatherer ancestry found in nearly all South Asians today ("Ancient Ancestral South Indian") trace back to these early migrants (Narasimhan et al. 2019).

## Daily life of early humans

Small bands of 20–50 people moved with the seasons, hunting, fishing and gathering hundreds of plant foods. They lived in caves, rock shelters and huts of branches, hides or mammoth bones. Life expectancy at birth was low because many children died, but adults often lived into their fifties and sixties. Studies of modern hunter-gatherers suggest a working week of perhaps 20–40 hours on food, with much time spent on social life, storytelling and ritual.
