Around **11,500 years ago**, soon after the Ice Age ended, people in the **Fertile Crescent** began cultivating wheat, barley and lentils and herding goats and sheep. Within a few thousand years farming had begun independently in **China** (rice, millet), **New Guinea** (taro, banana), **Mesoamerica** (maize, beans, squash), the **Andes** (potato, quinoa), **Africa** (sorghum, yams) and elsewhere.

## Before farming: Göbekli Tepe

At **Göbekli Tepe** in southeastern Turkey, hunter-gatherers raised T-shaped stone pillars, carved with foxes, boars, vultures and scorpions, around **9500 BCE**, centuries before pottery or farming in the area. Some archaeologists argue that the need to feed people at gatherings like these encouraged the cultivation of wild grain.

## Why people started farming

There is no single answer. Likely causes include a **warmer, stable climate**, **population growth**, the **collapse of big-game hunting** after megafauna extinctions, **feasting and social competition**, and plants gradually evolving under human harvesting.

## How it changed life

:::collapse !Gains and losses
**Gains:** reliable food surplus, permanent houses, pottery, weaving, larger populations, specialist crafts, and eventually cities, writing and states.

**Losses:** longer working hours; less varied diets; early farmers were often **shorter** and had more tooth decay and anaemia than hunter-gatherers. Living close to animals and in crowds brought **new diseases** (measles from cattle plague, influenza from birds, smallpox). Stored wealth made **inequality** and **war** worse.
:::

## Village life in the Neolithic

At **Çatalhöyük** (Turkey, ~7100–5700 BCE) several thousand people lived in mud-brick houses packed together without streets, entering through holes in the roofs. They buried their dead beneath the floors and decorated walls with bulls' horns and paintings.

At **Mehrgarh** (Balochistan, ~7000 BCE), South Asia's earliest known farmers grew barley and wheat, herded **humped zebu cattle** (domesticated locally), made beads of lapis lazuli and shell, and even drilled teeth, the earliest evidence of dentistry.

## India's Neolithic

Farming villages spread across South Asia: **Burzahom** in Kashmir (pit houses, ~3000 BCE), the ashmounds of the **southern Neolithic** in Karnataka and Andhra Pradesh (cattle pens burned in festivals), **Chirand** in Bihar, and early rice cultivation in the Ganga valley (**Lahuradewa**). Millets, pulses such as moong and urad, and later rice became staples, setting the stage for the [Indus civilisation](/wiki/ancient/indus-valley-civilisation.html).
