No one knows exactly when language began, because speech leaves no fossils. Clues come from anatomy, genes, archaeology and the languages themselves. Browse [language families and languages](/wiki/languages/index.html).

## When did speech begin?

- **Anatomy:** Neanderthals had a hyoid bone like ours and ear structures tuned to speech frequencies, so they probably could talk to some degree.
- **Genes:** the *FOXP2* gene, which affects fine control of speech, has the same human-specific version in Neanderthals.
- **Symbols:** engraved ochre (Blombos, ~75,000 years ago), beads and later art imply people who could share abstract ideas.
- Most researchers think **fully modern language existed by at least 100,000–50,000 years ago**, and possibly much earlier.

## Families of languages

About **7,000 languages** are spoken today in roughly **140 families**. The biggest:

| Family | Speakers | Homeland | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| Indo-European | ~3.3 billion | Pontic–Caspian steppe or Anatolia (debated) | Hindi, Bengali, English, Spanish, Russian, Persian |
| Sino-Tibetan | ~1.4 billion | Yellow River basin | Mandarin, Tibetan, Burmese, Meitei |
| Niger–Congo | ~700 million | West Africa | Yoruba, Swahili, Zulu |
| Afroasiatic | ~500 million | Northeast Africa / Levant | Arabic, Hebrew, Amharic, Hausa |
| Austronesian | ~380 million | Taiwan | Malay, Tagalog, Malagasy, Māori |
| Dravidian | ~250 million | South Asia | Tamil, Telugu, Kannada, Malayalam |
| Austroasiatic | ~120 million | Southeast Asia | Vietnamese, Khmer, Santali, Khasi |

India alone has languages from **four major families** (Indo-European, Dravidian, Austroasiatic, Sino-Tibetan) plus isolates such as **Nihali** and the Andamanese languages, and its 2011 census recorded **19,500+ mother tongues**.

## How languages change

:::collapse !Splits, sound shifts and borrowing
- **Divergence:** when groups separate, their speech drifts apart. Latin became Italian, Spanish, French and Romanian within about 1,000 years; Sanskrit's sister Prakrits gave rise to Hindi, Bengali and Marathi.
- **Regular sound changes:** linguists discovered patterns like **Grimm's law** (Latin *pater*, English *father*). In 1786 **Sir William Jones** noticed that Sanskrit, Greek and Latin share roots (Sanskrit *pitṛ*, Greek *patēr*, Latin *pater*), founding comparative linguistics.
- **Borrowing:** English took ~29% of its words from French and ~29% from Latin; Hindi–Urdu absorbed Persian and Arabic; Tamil and Sanskrit exchanged many words.
- **Contact languages:** traders created pidgins and creoles; Swahili grew from Bantu and Arabic.
- **Language death:** about **40% of languages are endangered**; the Great Andamanese languages now have only a few speakers.
:::

## From counting to writing

Writing was invented independently at least three or four times: **Mesopotamian cuneiform (~3200 BCE)** grew from clay tokens used to count grain and sheep; **Egyptian hieroglyphs** appeared around the same time; **Chinese** oracle-bone script ~1250 BCE; and the **Maya** script in Mesoamerica. The **Indus script** (~2600 BCE) remains undeciphered. The **alphabet** was invented once, by Semitic-speaking workers in Sinai around 1800 BCE, and spread through the Phoenicians to Greek, Latin, Aramaic, Arabic, Hebrew and, probably, India's **Brahmi**, the ancestor of almost every script from Devanagari to Thai.
