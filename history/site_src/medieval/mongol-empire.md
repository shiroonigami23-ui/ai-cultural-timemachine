In **1206** a council of steppe tribes proclaimed **Temüjin** as **Genghis Khan**, "universal ruler". Within 70 years his descendants ruled from **Korea to Hungary**, about **24 million km²**, the largest contiguous land empire ever.

## How they did it

- **Horse archers:** each warrior had several horses and could ride for days; composite bows outranged most enemies.
- **Organisation by tens** (arban, jaghun, mingghan, tumen) that broke up tribal loyalties.
- **Promotion by merit**, religious tolerance, and the use of captured engineers for siege warfare.
- **Terror:** cities that resisted, such as **Merv, Nishapur** and **Baghdad (1258)**, were massacred.

## The cost and the exchange

Estimates of deaths from the Mongol conquests reach **tens of millions**; Iran's irrigation systems and population took centuries to recover. Yet the **Pax Mongolica** made trade across Eurasia safer: **Marco Polo** travelled to Kublai Khan's court; the **yam** relay post system carried messages; and paper money, gunpowder, printing, foods and medical knowledge spread. So did the **Black Death**.

## The Mongols and India

The **Delhi Sultanate** repeatedly repelled Mongol invasions under **Alauddin Khalji** (1296–1316), sparing India the devastation of Persia. A descendant of both Genghis Khan and Timur, **Babur**, later founded the **Mughal** dynasty (from *Mughal*, Persian for "Mongol").

## Daily life on the steppe

Mongols lived in felt **gers** (yurts), moved with herds of horses, sheep, goats, yaks and camels, and ate meat and dairy: dried curds, butter and ***airag*** (fermented mare's milk). Women managed camps and herds and often held real power, such as Genghis's wife **Börte** and Kublai's mother **Sorghaghtani Beki**.
