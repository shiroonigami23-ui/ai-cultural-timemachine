In the 15th century the Ottomans controlled the land routes to Asia, so Europeans looked for sea routes to India's spices. Portugal pushed down Africa's coast; **Bartolomeu Dias** rounded the Cape of Good Hope (1488); **Christopher Columbus**, sailing west for Spain, reached the Caribbean (1492); **Vasco da Gama** reached **Calicut** in May **1498**; **Magellan and Elcano** completed the first circumnavigation (1519–22).

## The Columbian Exchange

Historian Alfred Crosby named the transfer of plants, animals, people and diseases between the Old and New Worlds the **Columbian Exchange**.

| Americas → Old World | Old World → Americas |
|---|---|
| Maize, potato, sweet potato, cassava, tomato, **chilli pepper**, peanut, cashew, pineapple, papaya, guava, cacao, vanilla, tobacco, rubber, turkey | Wheat, rice, sugarcane, coffee, bananas, citrus, horses, cattle, pigs, sheep, chickens, **smallpox, measles, malaria** |

Within a century, **chillies** had conquered Indian, Thai, Sichuan and Korean cooking; **potatoes** fuelled population booms in Europe (and Ireland's catastrophic dependence on them); **maize** and **sweet potato** fed China's growing population.

## The human cost

**Smallpox**, measles and flu killed perhaps **up to 90%** of the Indigenous peoples of the Americas within a century or so. The **Aztec** (1521) and **Inca** (1532) empires fell to small Spanish forces helped by disease and Indigenous allies. To work plantations and mines, Europeans shipped about **12.5 million enslaved Africans** across the Atlantic between 1526 and 1867. Silver from **Potosí** (Bolivia) flowed through Manila into China, creating the first global economy.

## In India

The Portuguese took **Goa** (1510), controlled sea trade by force with the *cartaz* pass system, and introduced chillies, cashew, papaya and pineapple. The Dutch, English, Danes and French followed, setting the stage for the [East India Company](/wiki/india/east-india-company-and-the-raj.html).
