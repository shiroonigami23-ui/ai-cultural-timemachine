After the **Abbasid** caliphs founded **Baghdad** in 762, the city became one of the largest in the world. Paper, learned from Chinese papermakers after the **Battle of Talas** (751), made books cheap.

## The House of Wisdom

The **Bayt al-Hikma** (House of Wisdom) in Baghdad sponsored translations of Greek (Aristotle, Euclid, Ptolemy, Galen), Persian and **Sanskrit** works into Arabic. The Indian astronomical text *Brahmasphutasiddhanta* was translated as the *Sindhind*, bringing Indian **numerals and zero** to the Arab world; from there they reached Europe as "Arabic numerals".

## Great minds

:::collapse !Scholars and discoveries
- **Al-Khwarizmi** (~780–850): *Al-Jabr* gave us **algebra**; his name gave us **algorithm**.
- **Al-Kindi**: philosophy and cryptanalysis (frequency analysis).
- **Al-Razi (Rhazes)**: distinguished smallpox from measles.
- **Ibn al-Haytham (Alhazen)**: *Book of Optics*, an early experimental scientist.
- **Al-Biruni**: measured Earth's radius; wrote the *Kitab al-Hind*, a detailed study of Indian science and society.
- **Ibn Sina (Avicenna)**: *Canon of Medicine*, used in European universities for 600 years.
- **Ibn Rushd (Averroes)**, in Cordoba: commentaries on Aristotle that shaped European thought.
- **Al-Zahrawi**: the father of surgery in al-Andalus.
- **Fatima al-Fihri** founded al-Qarawiyyin in Fez (859), often called the oldest continuously operating university.
:::

## Daily life

Cities had hospitals (*bimaristans*), public baths, paper mills and great markets. New crops spread across the Islamic world, sometimes called the **Arab Agricultural Revolution**: **rice, sugarcane, citrus, cotton, aubergine, spinach**. Coffee drinking began in Sufi circles of Yemen in the 15th century.

## The end

The **Mongol sack of Baghdad (1258)** destroyed the House of Wisdom; the Tigris was said to run black with ink. Learning continued in Cairo, Persia, Central Asia (Ulugh Beg's observatory, 1420s), the Ottoman Empire and Mughal India.
