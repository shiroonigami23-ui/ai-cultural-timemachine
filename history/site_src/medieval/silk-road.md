level: Introductory
---
The name **Silk Road** (*Seidenstraße*) was coined by German geographer Ferdinand von Richthofen in 1877. It was never one road but a web of caravan routes, linked to an even larger network of **sea lanes across the Indian Ocean**, active from about the **2nd century BCE (Han dynasty)** to the **15th century**.

## What travelled

| From | Goods |
|---|---|
| China | Silk, porcelain, tea, paper, later gunpowder and the compass |
| India | Cotton cloth, pepper and spices, indigo, gems, ivory, steel, sugar, and the numerals we use today |
| Central Asia | Horses (the "heavenly horses" of Ferghana), jade |
| Persia and Arabia | Carpets, glass, frankincense, dates |
| Rome / Byzantium | Gold and silver, glass, wine, coral |
| Africa | Gold, ivory, enslaved people |

## Ideas and faiths on the move

**Buddhism** travelled from India to Central Asia, China, Korea and Japan; pilgrims like **Faxian** (5th c.) and **Xuanzang** (7th c.) walked to India and studied at **Nalanda**. **Islam**, **Nestorian Christianity** and **Manichaeism** spread east; **Indian mathematics and astronomy** reached Baghdad and then Europe. Cities such as **Samarkand, Bukhara, Kashgar, Merv** and **Dunhuang** (with its Buddhist cave library) grew rich.

## The monsoon seaway

Sailors learned to ride the **monsoon winds**: southwest in summer toward India, northeast in winter back to Arabia and Africa. Ports such as **Muziris, Calicut, Cambay, Masulipatnam**, Aden, Hormuz, Malacca and Quanzhou linked a world of Arab, Gujarati, Tamil, Malay, Chinese and Swahili merchants. The Ming admiral **Zheng He** led giant treasure fleets to Calicut and East Africa (1405–1433).

## Dark cargo

Trade routes also spread disease. The **Black Death** travelled along Mongol caravan routes to Crimea and then by ship to Italy in 1347. See [Plagues and pandemics](/wiki/medieval/plagues-and-pandemics.html).
