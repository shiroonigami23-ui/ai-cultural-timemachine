In 1945 European empires still ruled much of Africa and Asia. Within 30 years almost all colonies were independent, with **India and Pakistan (1947)** leading the way, followed by Burma and Sri Lanka (1948), Indonesia (1949), Ghana (1957), most of Africa around **1960**, Algeria after a brutal war (1962), and the Portuguese colonies in 1974–75 (India took Goa in **1961**).

## The Cold War (1947–1991)

The USA and USSR competed for influence through alliances (NATO, Warsaw Pact), proxy wars (Korea, Vietnam, Afghanistan), a nuclear arms race and the **space race** (Sputnik 1957, Apollo 11 in 1969). India's **Jawaharlal Nehru**, Egypt's Nasser, Yugoslavia's Tito, Indonesia's Sukarno and Ghana's Nkrumah founded the **Non-Aligned Movement** (Belgrade, 1961). The Cold War ended with the fall of the **Berlin Wall (1989)** and the dissolution of the **USSR (1991)**.

## A transformed world

- **Population** grew from 2.5 billion (1950) to over 8 billion today.
- **Food:** the Green Revolution averted famine in Asia.
- **Health:** life expectancy worldwide rose from about 46 years (1950) to about 73.
- **Technology:** computers, the internet (1969/1989), mobile phones and now AI.
- **India:** from the 1951 elections to 1991 liberalisation, the IT boom, Chandrayaan-3 (2023) and becoming the world's most populous country (2023).

## Challenges of the 21st century

Climate change, inequality, pandemics (COVID-19), nuclear proliferation, misinformation and the governance of artificial intelligence.
