## World War I (1914–1918)

**Causes:** rival alliances (Germany and Austria-Hungary vs France, Russia and Britain), an arms race, nationalism and imperial competition. The spark was the assassination of **Archduke Franz Ferdinand** in Sarajevo on **28 June 1914**.

On the **Western Front**, trench warfare with machine guns, artillery and poison gas killed millions for a few kilometres of ground (Verdun, the Somme). **About 20 million** people died. The war destroyed four empires (German, Austro-Hungarian, Russian, Ottoman) and produced the **Russian Revolution** (1917) and the **League of Nations**.

**India's role:** about **1.3 million** Indian soldiers served in France, Mesopotamia, Gallipoli, Egypt and East Africa; around 74,000 died. Indian Victoria Cross recipients included **Khudadad Khan**. India Gate in Delhi commemorates them. Promises of self-government were broken, fuelling the 1919 protests.

## Between the wars

The **Great Depression** (from 1929), resentment of the Versailles Treaty and fear of communism helped **fascists** take power in Italy (Mussolini, 1922) and **Nazis** in Germany (Hitler, 1933). Japan invaded Manchuria (1931) and China (1937).

## World War II (1939–1945)

:::collapse !Main phases
- **1939–41:** Germany conquers Poland, France and much of Europe; the Battle of Britain.
- **1941:** Germany invades the Soviet Union; Japan attacks **Pearl Harbor**, bringing the USA into the war.
- **1942–43:** turning points at **Midway**, **El Alamein** and **Stalingrad**.
- **1944:** D-Day landings in Normandy; Soviet advance in the east; **Kohima and Imphal** stop Japan's invasion of India.
- **1945:** Germany surrenders (8 May); atomic bombs on **Hiroshima** (6 Aug) and **Nagasaki** (9 Aug); Japan surrenders (15 Aug, signed 2 Sept).
:::

About **70–85 million** people died, most of them civilians, including about **27 million Soviet citizens** and perhaps 15–20 million Chinese. In the **Holocaust** the Nazis systematically murdered **six million Jews**, along with Roma, disabled people, Poles, Soviet prisoners and others.

**India's role:** the British Indian Army grew to **2.5 million**, the largest volunteer army in history, fighting in North Africa, Italy and Burma. The **Bengal famine of 1943** killed 2–3 million people. Subhas Chandra Bose's **Indian National Army** fought alongside Japan.

## After the wars

The **United Nations** (1945), the Universal Declaration of Human Rights (1948), the **Cold War**, and a rapid wave of **decolonisation**, starting with India's independence in 1947.
